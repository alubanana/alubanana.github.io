Following a technology over time requires both research material and the reasoning behind earlier judgments. When a new result arrives, those records help identify what should change.

The [research radar](/projects/tech-research/) stores sources, experimental results and research judgments separately, and preserves versions during builds.

## Sources and dates

Each entry records the original link, event date and review date. The event date describes when research was published or an event occurred. The review date describes when the material was actually read.

The reading scope also distinguishes full text from abstract. It can be expanded when more material becomes available.

## Experimental results

Paper records include the task, subjects, sample and comparison baseline. Conclusions are interpreted within those conditions. Company demonstrations, independent tests and production deployment have distinct evidence types.

Keeping results and conditions together reduces omissions in summaries and makes studies within a direction easier to compare.

## Judgment records

Judgments record the event definition, supporting evidence, counterevidence and the next observation conditions. Original material and interpretation can be checked separately.

Changes in conditions or evidence trigger another review. Scenario calculations also retain their priors and scores so changes to the output can be explained.

## Versions

Builds retain question definitions, evidence, sources and judgments. A change creates a new version while earlier inputs remain available.

Comparing versions separates new evidence from changes in the question or human judgment. The public snapshot was updated through November 1, 2026; its full technical review, incremental checks and weekly scan retain their respective dates.
